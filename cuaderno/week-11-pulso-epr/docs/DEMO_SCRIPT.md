# Demo script - 3 minutes + 30 seconds

## 0:00–0:25 - The commercial question

Open <https://week-11-pulso-epr.vercel.app>.

“Pulso EPR does not calculate compliance or claim recycling. It answers a narrower Money question: before we buy or build another system, can we show recurring operational value against the routes that already exist?”

Point to the persistent warning and privacy row.

## 0:25–1:10 - Define and sense the cycle

Load the invented 24-SKU sample.

“These are aggregate invented inputs: SKU volume, changes, evidence completeness, exceptions, human hours, and cost. The phone is a sensor for workflow effort. I can time the work and mark reconciliation checkpoints, or use this clearly labeled simulated feed. It collects no files, identity, location, or free text.”

Switch briefly to measured mode; show that zero minutes keeps confidence exploratory. Return to simulated mode.

## 1:10–1:45 - Explain the ML layer

Scroll to the projection.

“The model returns a range, not a magic number. Version SYN-LIN-0.3 uses synthetic coefficients and exposes the three strongest drivers. It never claims production accuracy or high confidence. Its next test is to beat a simple first-cycle baseline on a real second cycle.”

Open “Ver fórmula y límites.”

## 1:45–2:30 - Compare routes

Scroll to the route cards.

“The third Dragon layer is marketplace logic. The user sets four hard conditions. A route passes only if it meets all four, and the system can return none. With this small sample, the partner-platform route passes the mathematical filter—but the card explicitly says this is not a recommendation and that price, Mexican coverage, and implementation still require real evidence.”

Change one constraint to show the no-winner state.

## 2:30–3:00 - Shadow and export

Scroll to the burden check.

“The shadow clause prevents a cheaper workflow from erasing supplier labor, paper evidence, or worker protections. Unsupported compliance, recycling, worker-benefit, and 10:1 claims are blocked. Export requires a human to review assumptions, and the result stays an aggregate local JSON file.”

Check the review box and export.

## 3:00–3:30 - What changed my mind

“I began the week thinking a new Mexican EPR product might be the opportunity. Benchmarking Recyda, Specright, and Lorax changed my mind: the first product should be a proof instrument, not another compliance platform. The meaningful result this week is not that one route wins. It is that the app can truthfully say none is proven, expose the hidden human work, and make a second comparable cycle the price of claiming recurring value.”
